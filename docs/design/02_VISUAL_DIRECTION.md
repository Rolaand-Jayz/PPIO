# Visual direction — stylized 3D at toy scale

## Established appearance
A warm, particular family home viewed through the scale and physical limitations of toys. Stylized manufactured characters and household materials with believable texture, edge wear, plastics, fabric, brushed metal, carpet pile, furniture joins and meaningful toy articulation. Atmosphere varies between innocence, vulnerability, the uncanny and a hard-earned return to warmth.

Energy references include adventurous, expressive toy-sized 3D action and character-forward JRPG presentation. External works are *tone references*, not character designs or asset sources. Avoid derivative silhouettes or unauthorized direct copies.

## Four silhouette contracts
Jayze: readable humanoid action figure plus weapon; Kip: low die-cast car chassis with real wheel/steering structure; Rallo: short powerful winged gargoyle figure; Jonnie: broad, repaired exoskeleton soldier with heavier body language.

## Art delivery
- Approved character concepts need consistent front/side/back/three-quarter views and **gameplay-distance** views.
- Concept art does not automatically establish final model geometry, size, costume or rig.
- Preserve real room adjacency and camera sightlines.
- Proxies for the Unknown and uncertain heirlooms must be visually neutral, clearly noncanonical.
- Godot builds layout, fonts, menus, input and interactive UI; avoid baked labels in generated UI images.
- Final assets require source .blend where applicable, .glb interchange, material maps, previews, rig/socket metadata, provenance and import validation.

Do not repurpose the prior SHMUP's pixel-art 16-bit craft roster as the current JRPG style guide.
