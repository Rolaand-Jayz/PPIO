# Narrative perspective, scene craft and dialogue standard

The camera and story are restricted by toy experience. **No CJ scene may show events the current toys could not reasonably witness or hear** from the current physical location. The rule applies equally to cutscenes, dialogue, gameplay observations, collectible memories and montage. If a CJ beat is emotionally essential, position it where toys could witness it or defer its revelation.

## Scene acceptance questions
1. Whose toy perspective is currently available, and where are they physically?
2. What can they actually perceive? What do they incorrectly infer?
3. What gameplay action or physical choice advances the scene?
4. What do they know before and after? Are they still allowed uncertainty?
5. What relationship change persists rather than being immediately resolved?
6. How is the scene's emotional meaning *shown* instead of explained?
7. If the scene is on the return journey, what specifically echoes or revises the outbound visit?

## Tone and restraint
The experience can be funny, theatrical, physically playful and adventure-driven; do not replace its heart with generic darkness, or turn every insight into a lecture. Ordinary family routines and acts of care should carry meaning. Stakes become meaningful because prior safety was genuine. Characters should not announce their arcs or state the game's themes like a thesis. Give each toy an individual movement and conversational rhythm.

## Canon boundary
Draft discovery may change noncritical staging, incidental dialogue, small visual business and order of presentation. It cannot silently change CJ's age, who witnessed an event, spatial connectivity, the later naming of Jonnie, family relationships, approved character arc endpoints or the Unknown's protected ambiguity. Use a recorded proposal for changes.
